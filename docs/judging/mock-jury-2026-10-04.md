## 1. Tomasz Wróbel — critical Polish entrepreneur

**Profile.** 52, serial founder. Sold govtech SaaS to about 40 gminas through PZP tenders and exited one company. He has sat through 200 pitches and trusts none of them.

**First 30 seconds.** "A cartoon signed 'by me age 10' with the words 'caste system' on it. Then two problems. Then three Hungarians telling me what Kraków needs. Why are you wasting my first minute?"

**What works**
- The "first 24 hours" framing on slide 10 is the only sentence a Polish wójt would repeat to the voivode. It should open the deck, not close it.
- Alert RCB moving into mObywatel is the right hook: "offline continuation of Alert RCB" is a sellable one-liner.
- You built something since yesterday. Respect.

**Problems**
1. **[Blocker] Still no buyer.** Who signs the invoice? A gmina cannot order Orange to flash routers, cannot issue certificates, cannot touch mObywatel. Slide 10 says "pilot in one district with one ISP": which district, which ISP, which budget line? Without a name, this is a wish.
2. **[Blocker] No ask, no money.** No cost per router, per resident or per city, and no ISP compensation. The whole thesis is "uses what's already there", so that slide would be cheap and persuasive, and it is missing.
3. **[Major] Payments are still in** (slide 3, slide 6 "2 PLN"). The state running a paid lending marketplace competes with OLX and Allegro Lokalnie, and private rental income is taxed from the first złoty. Yesterday's jury said drop it. You kept it.
4. **[Major] Liability.** An untrained volunteer runs an AED to a prank. Daily OK escalates "to 112". There is no CPR/dispatcher integration, no Art. 66 KW story and no DPIA. In Poland, AED guidance is the dispatcher's job; you are building a parallel dispatcher.
5. **[Major] Pre-event work.** Your git log shows 15k lines before 23:00. If the 11 PM in the rules is literal and a competitor checks, you are out. Put one line in the submission stating the start time.
6. **[Minor] Name.** "Pomóc" is an infinitive, unregistrable, with a diacritic in the brand. Fine for a hackathon, but don't fall in love with it.

**Q&A he would ask**
- "Name the first customer and what they pay in year one."
- "Why would Orange agree to put your firmware on 3 million CPEs? What's in it for them?"
- "Who is liable when your network sends a neighbour into a flat that turns out to be a burglary?"

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 → 21 | 5 → 10 | 5 → 10 | 6 → 12 | 6 → 6 | **59** |

**Verdict.** "A good research project wearing a startup's clothes. Cut payments, name a crisis office and an ISP, and show me a number."

## 2. Kuba Lis — young engineer

**Profile.** 26, backend and mobile engineer at a Kraków startup. Runs a Meshtastic node on his balcony and reads the code before the slides.

**First 30 seconds.** Clones the repo and runs `pnpm test`, which shows 278 green. Opens the sim and drags a phone across the Vistula. "Oh, this is actually nice."

**What works**
- **Engine/UI split is clean.** `packages/core` is a pure, deterministic engine with seeded PRNG and determinism pinned by tests. That is genuinely good engineering for 24 hours.
- **The traced Kraków street graph plus walk/bike/car mobility** makes store-and-forward visible. Packets moving one hop per tick sell the idea better than any slide.
- **The forged-request demo (`f`) and per-node congestion drops** show protocol thinking, not just animation.
- The honest-scope box on slide 9 is the right instinct.

**Problems**
1. **[Blocker] False D rows.** Topology gossip does not exist: adjacency is computed globally from geometry (`tick.ts:106`) and the `t` "gossip traffic" toggle shows nothing. Rate limiting is a no-op. AED matching is "fewest hops", not a registered resource. If a technical judge diffs `functional.md` against the code, every false D costs you more than an honest P would.
2. **[Major] The demo does not show your best slide.** Slide 4 (AED ~40 m, "guides Marek to the AED") is the emotional peak, but the sim has no AED and no resource. Add one static resource node and a "nearest capable responder" rule. That is a few hours of work and makes slide 4 demonstrable.
3. **[Major] No screenshot in the deck.** You have a beautiful map, and slide 9 is still text pills. Replace the pills with a screenshot or a 10-second GIF.
4. **[Major] Timescale breaks the story.** L1 triggers after 1 s instead of 10 min, declarations last 60 s and TTL is 24–60 s, while the deck says "expires in 2 h" and the concept says store-and-forward "for hours". At 60 s TTL a walker carries a message about 40 m, so all the muling is done by cars. Either label the sim as compressed time or scale TTLs.
5. **[Minor] Crypto stub.** WebCrypto Ed25519 runs in Chrome and Safari. Signing at origin and verifying at first hop is about 50 lines and would let you say "forgeries are rejected by a real signature check".
6. **[Minor] Scale claim.** NFR-PERF-07 says 20k routers and 2k phones, but the UI caps at 10k and there is no performance test. Show an FPS number or drop the claim.
7. **[Minor] Unregistered phones relay in peace mode** in the code, while the concept says emergency only (FR-ID-08).

**Q&A he would ask**
- "Is the topology the nodes see learned or omniscient?" (Today it is omniscient. Say so before he finds out.)
- "What happens to iOS background BLE? Your phone-only tier is basically Android."
- "Show me the percolation threshold. Where's the curve?"

## 3. Inż. Andrzej Kowalczyk — old engineer

**Profile.** 64, 35 years at TP S.A., later Orange Polska, in network operations. He ran field ops during the 1997 flood and the 2024 Kłodzko flood. Was a radio amateur before anyone had a mobile phone.

**First 30 seconds.** "Home routers. On mains power. In a blackout. Go on, I'm listening."

**What works**
- You found the real failure order: cells fail first, then power, then batteries. The degradation ladder (`04-degradation-ladder.mmd`) is the first time I have seen students draw it correctly.
- Gateways on satellite or generator as "the mesh's door to the Authority" is how we actually ran 1997: islands plus couriers. You re-invented the courier as a data mule, and you model it.
- Store-and-forward by people walking is not a gimmick. It is what worked when everything else did not.

**Problems**
1. **[Blocker] Power.** In a city-wide blackout about 95% of CPE goes dark within seconds; there is no battery in a Funbox. Your backbone exists exactly in the scenario (L1, cell outage with mains on) that matters least. The sim's `g` key shows it, so be honest on the slide: "routers carry L1; L2 blackout is phones and battery-backed relays." Then the coverage-planning vision becomes the real product.
2. **[Major] 802.11s on ISP CPE.** Broadcom and Realtek vendor firmware does not ship it, so every CPE model needs firmware work from a vendor that does not care. TR-369 coverage across Polish ISPs is patchy outside the big four. Quote a real fleet number or say "Orange Funbox 6 only, for the pilot".
3. **[Major] Radio reality.** BLE in a pre-war kamienica with 60 cm brick walls carries 5–10 m, not the disc radius in the sim. Your percolation claims depend on that one parameter. Put a range sensitivity on slide 9: "at 15 m BLE we need X phones/km², Kazimierz has Y."
4. **[Major] Flooding a 15-hop region at top priority** with a 30-day pseudonymous certificate and per-node token buckets: N receivers each allow the full quota. One bad actor with a valid cert saturates a district. Offline revocation is still missing.
5. **[Minor] Timescale.** "Expires in 2 h" against a 60 s TTL. An engineer notices within a minute.
6. **[Minor] Replay across islands.** A timestamp check at the first admitting node only cannot be verified downstream. An old LIFE_CRITICAL replayed into another island looks like a muled one.

**Q&A he would ask**
- "How many of the routers in your Kraków map have a battery?"
- "What is your assumed BLE range, and what happens to delivery if I halve it?"
- "Who answers the phone when your mesh band ruins grandma's Wi-Fi?"

| 6 → 18 | 6 → 12 | 5 → 10 | 6 → 12 | 6 → 6 | **58** |

## 4. Zbigniew Nowak — always the pessimist

**Profile.** 58, risk and compliance consultant for public-sector IT. He has seen three national apps launch and two of them die. Always assumes the worst, and is right more often than he would like.

**First 30 seconds.** "Every message in a disaster is a rumour vector, and every certificate is a target. Every volunteer is a liability claim. Convince me otherwise."

**What works** (he lists these reluctantly)
- Turning citizen info off at L3 for rumour control is the one thing that shows you have thought about the bad actor, not just the good neighbour.
- Relay certificates that "carry, never ask" is a sound cryptographic rule.
- Switching to device-generated keys removes yesterday's headline risk.

**Problems**
1. **[Blocker] Adoption zero.** Nobody installs a neighbourhood-sharing app for a drill: Peerby itself shrank. If peace mode fails, the network does not exist on the bad day. Your bad-day value depends entirely on a peacetime product with no evidence of retention.
2. **[Blocker] Misuse.** A prank LIFE_CRITICAL floods 15 hops at top priority. Daily OK sends strangers to a senior's door. "Casualty report with voluntary position" is a targeting beacon in L3, the exact level where you say phones go quiet. One incident ends the project.
3. **[Major] The state holds the social graph.** Gossiped router locations plus pseudonymous hop distances plus an Authority that issues every certificate makes a surveillance tool. "Complies with GDPR" is not a DPIA. Post-Pegasus Poland will read the headline before the architecture.
4. **[Major] Jamming and spoofing.** Jamming can "force L1 at most", but forcing L1 still flips every router into relay mode across a district. That is a free DoS lever.
5. **[Major] Overclaim risk.** About 20 requirement rows say "demonstrated" for things that are not in the code. The rules say misleading the jury about scope "may result in disqualification". Combined with undisclosed pre-23:00 commits, that is two independent ways to get disqualified, both fixable in 20 minutes.
6. **[Minor] "Every piece is tried and validated"** (slide 10). Several pieces are not: 802.11s on CPE and iOS background BLE relay. One judge with a phone in hand will disprove it on stage.

**Q&A he would ask**
- "What's your worst-case incident, and who goes on TV to explain it?"
- "How many people must already have the app for Kazimierz to work, and how long does that take?"
- "What stops the Authority from switching on L3 for a protest?"

## 5. Agnieszka Zielińska — open-minded community builder

**Profile.** 41, runs a social-innovation NGO and a community centre in Nowa Huta, and coordinated volunteer help for Ukrainian refugees in 2022. She judges by whether her neighbours would use it.

**First 30 seconds.** "I love the cartoon. I love 'the city already owns what it needs; it is in the wrong pocket.' Now show me a person, not a protocol."

**What works**
- **The dual-use idea is genuinely humane.** The trust built on Tuesday is what saves you on the bad day. That is how real community resilience works; in 2022 it was neighbours on Facebook groups, not institutions.
- **Daily OK has the right escalation order**: the people the senior chose, then neighbours, then 112. It is opt-in and stays on the device. This is the most emotionally resonant feature and the jury will remember it.
- **"Local questions and answers"** (is the pharmacy open, is the playground flooded) is more valuable day-to-day than lending drills.
- Slide 4's four-step AED story is clear and moving.

**Problems**
1. **[Major] The people are invisible in the demo.** Ola and Marek live on slide 4 and nowhere else. In the sim they are dots. Name two dots "Ola" and "Marek" and run the AED story live. Same code, ten times the impact.
2. **[Major] Who is left out?** The phone-only tier works on Android but iPhone only in the foreground. Seniors without smartphones, people without mObywatel, and foreigners without PESEL are 10–20% of a Kraków district. The router portal helps, so say so, and say how a babcia without a phone reaches it.
3. **[Major] No local partners.** No OSP, Straż Miejska, parish, housing cooperative, rada dzielnicy or Centrum Zarządzania Kryzysowego. Communities adopt through trusted local institutions, not app stores. One slide naming "pilot with the rada dzielnicy of Kazimierz and the local OSP" changes how this feels.
4. **[Minor] Tone.** "Caste system" (slide 1), "terror, war" (slide 5) and "fewer casualties" (slide 10) skew militaristic for a Smart City pitch. Lead with the neighbour, and let the war case be the reserve.
5. **[Minor] Payments** undermine the neighbourly spirit. A drill for 2 PLN feels like OLX. Credits or gratitude would fit better.
6. **[Minor] There is no team slide.** Three faces and one line on why you care would help, especially as a non-Polish team pitching a Polish brand. Own it: "we're Hungarian, DÁP is our mObywatel, this works in any EU wallet" is a strength.

**Q&A she would ask**
- "How would my 80-year-old neighbour with a push-button phone take part?"
- "Who in the neighbourhood would champion this, and why would they trust it?"
- "Have you talked to a single person who would use it?"
